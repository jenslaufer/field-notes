# Biased consensus — a debate is an amplifier, and the gain is set by sampling temperature

The claim, and it is a mechanism rather than an observation: a multi-agent debate is a
**spin system**. Each agent has a private bias `h` and a conformity coefficient `λ`; sampling
temperature `T` is the noise. Below a critical noise level, an arbitrarily small shared bias is
amplified into unanimity — a phase transition, rounded into a crossover because the number of
agents `N` is finite. Above it, the same bias stays a bias.

They then measure it, on 11 models across five families (OpenAI, DeepSeek, Llama, Mistral, Qwen,
including base vs RLHF variants), and it behaves as predicted.

**The applied result (Fig. 1, before any theory):**

| Task | bias measured | what happens at T < 0.5 |
|---|---|---|
| Investment recommendation, 10× GPT-4.1 Nano | US/tech concentration | biased consensus locks in **within 1–2 rounds**; market-adjusted return falls |
| LLM-as-a-judge on MT-Bench, 6 agents | self-preference (GPT-3.5 favouring GPT-3.5) | same, and agreement with human labels falls |

`ΔT ≈ 0.1` is enough to cross the transition. Both realistic tasks show bias *up* and performance
*down* at low temperature — the two curves move against each other, and the round-by-round plots
show the lock-in happening in the first two exchanges.

## The two mitigations, both cheap

1. **Heterogeneous sampling temperatures.** Not "raise T" — *mix* it. Six agents at
   `T ∈ {1.4, 1.5, 1.6}` (investment) and ten at `T ∈ {1.3 … 1.7}` (judge), two per value.
   Against the homogeneous-temperature baseline this **reduces bias and improves performance**
   on both realistic tasks. The mechanism is in the theory: composing several different response
   functions smooths the collective response and rounds the transition off.
2. **Mixed model families.** Three agents at `T = 1.2`: homogeneous populations (3× GPT-4.1,
   3× Llama-405B, 3× DeepSeek) reach `m(R) ∈ [−0.7, 0.5]`; the mixed population reaches
   `[−0.5, 0.2]`. Same smoothing mechanism, and the mean-field prediction fitted on the
   homogeneous runs predicts the mixed result.

Also modulating the effective field: **sparse interaction topology** (each agent sees fewer
neighbours) and higher **top-p**. Both weaken the lock-in.

## The diagnostic worth stealing

Fit the one-step transition `m(t) → m(t+1)` from an observed debate trace and you recover each
model's **conformity `λ`** and **bias `h`** separately — no new experiment, just the trajectory
you already ran. Their fits land the families in different places: Llama and Qwen-base near
`h ≈ 0` with positive `λ` (little bias, real conformity); DeepSeek the strongest positive `λ`;
**GPT variants at `λ < 0`** — *anti*-conformity — with wider spread in bias direction. Instruct
variants often depart from their base models, so alignment tuning moves the coefficient.

That last point is a caution, not a fact to carry: `λ < 0` for GPT-4.1 here sits directly against
[`2605.10698`](2605.10698-bystander.md), where GPT-5.4 is the sycophant that folds at n=2 while
Claude does not. Different generations, different protocols — the honest reading is that
**conformity is a per-model, per-version coefficient that has to be measured, not a property of
a vendor.**

## Where it does not reach

**The controlled tasks have no right answer, by construction.** *"Because there are no correct
answers in our tasks, any non-neutral consensus reflects bias."* Agents pick between two neutral
symbols, or assign a task to Jane or John. This is the correct design for isolating the
amplifier — and it means the paper never measures what the amplifier does when the initial lean
is *toward the truth*. Its own mechanism is direction-agnostic: low `T` locks in whatever the
population starts leaning at. On a task with a checkable answer, the same physics says a debate
that starts mostly-right converges to right, and one that starts mostly-wrong converges to
wrong, faster and more confidently in both cases. That is the same statement as the co-failure
ceiling in [`2606.27288`](2606.27288-co-failure-ceiling.md), arrived at from the other side.

**"Performance improves at high temperature" does not generalise off these two tasks.** The
performance metrics are market-adjusted portfolio return and agreement with human MT-Bench
labels — tasks where *the bias is the error*. Removing the lock-in removes the error. On a task
where the answer is derived rather than preferred, `T = 1.5` costs accuracy directly and the
trade runs the other way. The paper does not claim otherwise, but the sentence "heterogeneous
ensembles reduce bias and improve performance" travels badly on its own.

**An internal inconsistency in the ablation.** The Fig. 4 caption lists *"(c) confidence
visibility (λᵢ↓)"*; the body text describing the same panel says *"(c) confidence visibility …
**increasing** λ"* — opposite signs for the same knob. And panel (d) is *"prompting that
increases sycophancy (λᵢ↑)"*, after which the text concludes *"Overall, all four factors
mitigate the emergence of a collective norm, consistent with our theory."* Under the paper's own
model, a collective norm emerges **when conformity λ exceeds a critical threshold** — so a
factor that raises λ cannot mitigate emergence. Either the caption's signs or the summary
sentence is wrong. The figures are images in this rendering, so which one cannot be settled from
the HTML.

Stated limitations, fairly: binary discretisation of high-dimensional outputs, a simplified
protocol (short-term memory, all-to-all or random topology, no expert roles), and four tasks.

## The collision with the previous paper in this list

[`2605.10698`](2605.10698-bystander.md) ran **every one of its 22,500 trajectories at T = 0**,
*"to ensure maximal-likelihood reasoning paths and absolute reproducibility"*, and named the open
question itself: whether `T > 0.7` would let a model escape the alignment hallucination.

This paper answers it in the affirmative and identifies `T = 0` as **the maximally conformist
regime** — the precise corner of the phase diagram where an arbitrarily small bias becomes
unanimity. The Bystander paper's central design choice for reproducibility is the condition that
maximises the effect it measures. Neither paper cites the other; read together, the Bystander
constants (`D_L ≈ 2`) are upper bounds on fragility at zero noise, not the operating point.
