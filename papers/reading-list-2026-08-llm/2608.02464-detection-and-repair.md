# The paper measured its own assumption, watched it destroy its headline, and printed both

2,823 agent episodes, three frameworks, three local models across two families, plus
gemini-2.5-flash. A one-class monitor over step telemetry — semantic embedding, token
uncertainty, action metadata — trained on **healthy runs only**, ~200 µs per step against one
7B forward pass for a judge that reads every step. That is the paper's stated subject.

Its actual result is somewhere else, and the author says so: **the deterministic check beats the
monitor it took the whole paper to build.**

## The head-to-head, same episodes and same labels

| | deterministic checks | statistical monitor |
|---|---|---|
| failures caught, T=0.2 (served) | **60 %** (96 % with the coverage check) | 54 % |
| false positives, T=0.2 | **0 / 63 = 0 %** | 11 / 63 = **17 %** |
| failures caught, T=0.9 | 65 % (96 %) | 40 % |
| false positives, T=0.9 | 0 / 38 = 0 % | 6 / 38 = 16 % |

The checks are three, and none of them is clever:

- `total_consistency` — recompute the run's stated total from the tool results **that run
  actually received**.
- `required_coverage` — confirm every call the task requires was made.
- `tool_contract` — does this result match any shape its tool can return? Reports at the step the
  result arrives, not at the answer.

None reads the hidden world the task was generated from, so all three ship as-is. **They need no
healthy null, no threshold, no calibration** — which is the monitor's entire operating burden.

Held out honestly: the checks were written by inspecting failures in the serving arm, so 120
further episodes at disjoint task seeds were collected afterwards and scored **frozen** — 54 %
by totals, 93 % with coverage, arithmetic errors 36/36, **0 of 64 false positives**. Transfer to
llama3.1:8b with nothing retuned: **110 of 110 at 0 of 10**. Provoked fabrications: **26 of 26**
— the class a one-class monitor structurally cannot even be scored on (provoking enough
fabrication leaves 2 healthy episodes against the 15 a null needs). `tool_contract` trips on
**0 of 1825** healthy episodes and fires within one step of onset in 215 of 218 cases.

## The assumption that ate the headline

§6's escalation result — "recovers 83 % of judge-every-step detection at 8 % of the calls" — was
computed against a **stipulated** judge: p_detect 0.90, p_false 0.02. The author then measured a
real gemini-2.5-flash judge on 161 labelled prompts:

> **p_detect 0.548** (95 % CI 0.44–0.65), **p_false 0.052** — *both stipulated values lie outside
> their measured intervals.*

Substituting the measured rates and re-running all five master seeds with everything else fixed:
the cost saving survives (6 % vs 8 % of calls), but detection recovery falls from **82 % to
43 %**, and the hypothesis is **supported at 0 of 5 seeds**. That paragraph is in the paper,
under its own subheading, and the abstract carries the corrected number.

This is the discipline [[proof with numbers]] keeps asking for, executed against the author's own
interest. It is also the fifth independent instance in this reading list of the same lesson:
Pivotal Vote's "just run the tests" beat every panel policy; Small/Free/Effective put 11.17 pp on
deterministic evidence and 0.26 pp on the LLM verifier; Calibration Floor showed a critic with no
outside evidence changes nothing; Wix's gate needs no labelled dataset at all. **Five papers, four
research groups, one instruction: run the deterministic check first, and only escalate what it
cannot answer.**

## The result I did not expect, and will change how I write repair prompts

Every flagged run is rolled back to its last fact-gathering step and re-run live. Five repair
rungs against one resampling control, n=55, three repeats each:

| rung | recovery | vs resample | calls / recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| untouched | 0 % | — | — |
| resample (rollback + fresh sample) | 16 % | control | 14.7 |
| **`located` — name the failing check, no values** | **45 %** | **p = 0.0005** | **6.4** |
| `generic` — "please re-check" | 36 % | p = 0.0347 | 5.8 |
| `specific` — the finding, **with** the values | 36 % | p = 0.0192 | 8.1 |
| `recompute` — "use the calculator" | 28 % | p = 0.17 (n.s.) | 7.2 |
| adaptive | 21 % | p = 0.61 (n.s.) | 10.6 |

**Telling the model *which check failed* and nothing else beats handing it the finding.** And it
is not close on cost either — 6.4 calls per recovery against 8.1. The author nails down why: 26
of the 55 `specific` hints **contain the correct total outright**, while `located` states no value
at all and still recovers more. So the recovery is not "being handed the answer" — it is being
pointed at the place and made to re-derive.

That is the Bystander finding (`2605.10698`) from the other side. There, one sentence asserting a
conclusion drove GPT-5.4 from 1.00 to 0.23. Here, withholding the conclusion and supplying only
the location outperforms supplying it. **Same rule, twice measured, opposite experiments: give a
checking agent the coordinates, never the verdict.** I have been doing the opposite in every
"prüfe diesen Befund" prompt I write.

Net over all 120 episodes, charging each policy for any correct run it broke: `located` lifts task
success **52 % → 73 %**, 25 failures recovered, **zero correct runs broken** (the checks flagged
no already-correct episode). Retry luck is controlled — plain resampling recovers 16 %, and only
the margin above it is credited. And Bonferroni is applied to the author's own five rungs at
α = 0.01: **only `located` survives**; `generic` and `specific` are demoted to "suggestive rather
than established" in the paper's own text.

## Where the monitor genuinely does not work — stated as measurements, not caveats

- **No cross-deployment transfer.** Calibrated on qwen2.5:7b, deployed on llama3.1:8b over a
  matched task/tool/injector plan: **AUROC 0.527, healthy false-alarm 0.75** — chance. The same
  target recalibrated on itself: **0.885**. The healthy null must be collected under the exact
  serving distribution: temperature, toolset, probe pattern, latency regime.
- **Organic (non-injected) failures defeat it.** Monitors carrying their deployed calibration
  catch **1 of 3** fabrications and rank the organic failure set **at or below chance** (AUROC
  0.31–0.42) while false-alarming on **36 %** of healthy episodes. Reported as not deployable.
- **The pre-registered fabrication study is underpowered and makes no claim.** 2 hallucinations
  against a pre-registered minimum of 10 (9 across all 175 organic episodes, only 2 fabricated
  inputs). Conclusion printed verbatim: *"These well-aligned agent models rarely invent figures."*
  The class is handed to the deterministic grounding verifier instead.
- **Silent aborts are invisible to telemetry and trivial otherwise** — a **five-line completion
  check** ("is the final step empty or a bare tool call?") catches **7 / 7** at 0 false positives.
- **Logprobs are not needed.** Ablating the whole token-surprisal channel moves AUROC by
  **+0.000** and detection by +0.002. The deployment requirement is *structured tool results*, not
  model internals.
- **The reservoir is not the contribution.** §6: the per-channel max wrapper carries most of the
  margin — "giving a GRU the same wrapper lifts it past our ESN". The ESN is just the cheap way to
  pay for the wrapper (1.7 s closed-form fit vs 68 s).
- **Judge and monitor fail on *different* classes** — the judge is perfect on goal drift (21/21)
  and nearly blind on context corruption (0.18); the monitor is 1.00 on context corruption. That,
  not cost, is the real case for escalation as a layer.

## What I take into this setup

1. **Write the three checks, not the watchdog.** `total_consistency` (does the number in the
   report trace to a tool result this run actually received?), `required_coverage` (was every
   required call made?), `tool_contract` (does this result have a shape its source can produce?).
   All three are ordinary code, need no baseline, and here beat a calibrated statistical monitor
   on both recall and precision. My own equivalents already exist in fragments —
   `deadline-check.py`, `context-budget.py`, `cal-availability-check.py` — and the missing one is
   the first: **no figure in a Telegram report that does not trace to a fetched result.**
   [[proof with numbers]] [[unrunnable check]]
2. **Point, don't tell.** In any verify/repair prompt: name the failing check and the location,
   withhold the value and the verdict. Measured here at 45 % vs 36 % recovery and 6.4 vs 8.1
   calls; measured in Bystander as a 1.00 → 0.23 collapse in the other direction.
3. **A monitor's calibration is part of its deployment, not part of its code.** Anything I tune
   against one model, one temperature, one toolset is at chance on the next one — 0.527 vs 0.885
   is the price, on a *matched plan*.
4. **"Absent" and "undetected" are different claims** — Figure 2 leaves cells blank rather than
   scoring zero where a corpus does not contain a class. Same distinction as
   [[unrunnable check]] and Exit 126.
