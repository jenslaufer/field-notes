# The Calibration Floor — most measured "self-correction" is a regex finding a number

The claim, and it is aimed at a whole literature: when a model reviews its own answer and the
score moves, that movement is read as *reasoning*. A large share of it is **whether the answer
could be extracted from the free-form text at all**. Generation runs out of token budget
before the answer marker; the review prompt invites a longer re-derivation; the model echoes
the placeholder `#### <answer>` instead of filling it in. Any accuracy pipeline scores an
unextractable answer as wrong — so an extraction failure is arithmetically identical to a
reasoning error, and the difference is invisible.

## The decomposition

Every (initial, revised) answer pair falls into exactly one of four boxes: **bothok** (both
parseable), **recover** (initial unparseable, revision parseable), **loss** (the reverse),
**dead** (neither). That gives an exact, additive identity over the whole sample:

```
Δ_total = Δ_content + Δ_format-recover + Δ_format-loss
```

Only `Δ_content` can be attributed to the model changing its mind. The paper is scrupulous
about what this is not: **bothok membership depends on the revision's own outcome**, so it is
a post-treatment-selected subgroup. The algebra is exact regardless; reading `Δ_content` as
"the reasoning effect" is the step that isn't.

## Six independent checks, all pointing the same way

29 admitted cells, Qwen3.5 0.8B–9B + Gemma-4-12B, plus a frontier arm.

**1. Sign reversal on identical cells.** Run the same (model, task) under a truncation-prone
prompt and a repaired one and the headline flips: 2B×GSM8K goes **−0.213 → −0.017**,
2B×MMLU **−0.053 → +0.080**. In every row the format margin tracks the total; the content
margin barely moves. Under bad prompts the *revision* fails to emit an answer (apparent harm,
consistent with the famous Huang et al. negative result); under repaired prompts the failures
sit on the *initial* answer, so the revision's second chance at emitting a string reads as
repair.

**2. The statistic.** On the 12 cells with ≥5 % unparseable initial answers, |Δ_format| beats
|Δ_content|, median difference **+0.055**, one-sided Wilcoxon **p = 1.7 × 10⁻³**
(BH-adjusted 2.6 × 10⁻³).

**3. The cheapest diagnostic in the paper, and the most damning result.** Take rows whose
initial answer didn't parse and force a 32-token greedy continuation of the *original*
response — `"Therefore, my final answer is"`, no new turn, no invitation to re-reason. On
4B GSM8K this recovers a correct answer on **63.5 %** of those rows. **The full two-round
review protocol recovers 19.2 %.** The expensive self-correction does worse than simply asking
the model to finish its sentence. Two cells (4B MMLU, 4B CommonsenseQA) go the other way and
are reported as honest counter-examples.

**4. The causal control.** Freeze the already-generated reasoning text and re-extract the
answer under **grammar-constrained decoding**, so parseability is guaranteed. All 14 cells
reach 0 % unparseable. Median closure of the gap between the naive total and the content
estimate: **71 %** (range 55–100 %, two cells exact). The grid's single largest apparent gain,
9B MMLU, falls **+0.145 → +0.053**; 0.8B MMLU flips sign **+0.020 → −0.017**. The residual on
4B/9B MMLU is stated, not dismissed: "C1's qualitative claim (format is the primary driver)
survives; its quantitative claim (a specific, universal percentage) does not, and we report
the range rather than collapse it to one number."

**5. Wording moves format, not content.** A review template that invites re-deriving each
option drives MMLU's format margin **+0.105 → −0.010** — a full sign flip — while the content
margin stays within 0.02 across all four variant/task combinations.

**6. Cross-family, and it gets worse with scale.** Gemma-4-12B: Δ_total +0.005 / +0.040 /
+0.030 on GSM8K / MMLU / MATH — **Δ_content exactly 0.000 on all three**, content change rate
0.000. Of MMLU's 12 "changed" rows, every single one is an unparseable-initial row. On the
frontier arm, Hy3 × MMLU posts **Δ_total +0.275 with Δ_content 0.000**.

## The literature replication, and why the failure is funny

They re-ran Li et al.'s published IoE confidence-gating protocol *verbatim* from its released
code, on GSM8K:

| | Standard | +IoE | +IoE+Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| reported (gpt-3.5-turbo-0613) | 74.9 % | 77.1 % | **78.5 %** |
| Qwen3.5-4B | 81.8 % | 81.5 % | 81.5 % |
| Qwen3.5-9B | 89.5 % | 85.5 % | **88.2 %** — below standard |

Δ_content: 0.000 (4B), +0.005 (9B). The small net-negative totals are format-loss — **traced
to Qwen's habit of writing `##` for markdown headers, which collides with the protocol's own
`##X##` answer delimiter.** A published method's measured gain, undone by a punctuation
collision with the model's formatting style. (They are careful: the original snapshot was
retired by OpenAI in 2024, so nobody can reproduce the original number; this is a
protocol-fidelity replication on a family it wasn't designed for, not a refutation.)

## Scale, and the squeeze

Floor-scale models (0.8B/2B) *do* change their minds — 5 of 6 cells show 7.4–19.9 % content
change rate versus 0.0–3.1 % at capable scale — and it is **net harmful**. Clustered GEE: 15.9×
higher odds of a content change, **21.0×** higher odds of a harmful flip.

Then the part worth copying more than the result: **they refuse their own p-value.** With only
4 distinct checkpoints, an exact permutation test over tier assignment has a hard floor of
**p = 0.333** and a 3-task sign test floors at p = 0.25. They report those floors instead of
the GEE's asymptotic p < 10⁻⁷, and label the finding "a clear difference across the
checkpoints evaluated here, not a general scaling law."

The squeeze (C3): floor-scale cells have real headroom but no signal that clears the
calibration floor; capable-scale cells have signal but **the maximum 95 % CI upper bound on
content headroom across all 19 capable cells is 0.036** — even perfectly selective revision
could not buy 4 accuracy points. Exactly one cell lands in the viable quadrant (9B TriviaQA),
and its prespecified sealed policy realises **+0.005** on holdout with an oracle-regret
fraction of 0.83 (target < 0.50) and no edge over a random gate at the same trigger rate.

## Two more marks of an unusually honest paper

- **They publish their own bug.** A coauthor code review found the empirical-ROC routine had a
  threshold-walk direction error that collapsed the curve to the two points (0,0) and (1,1) —
  which trivially matches the fitted curve's endpoints and produced a **spuriously near-perfect
  R² = 0.999–1.000** in an earlier draft. Fixed, everything regenerated, honest fit
  R² = 0.898–0.985. A near-perfect fit was the *symptom*.
- **They check the grader can't explain it.** Re-grading MATH with a computer-algebra checker
  flips 92/326, 86/313 and 54/185 rows from wrong to right and **none the other way** — the
  string grader undercounts badly. Yet Δ_content is unchanged to three decimals, because the
  conservatism applies symmetrically to both sides of the comparison.

Stated limits: not formally preregistered (the extraction gate was added *post hoc* after an
audit, and every analysis depending on the admitted-cell set inherits that); the causal control
identifies only how much the effect shrinks under guaranteed parseability, not whether the
free-form text already carried the answer; benchmark contamination unaddressed; one checkpoint
per scale point, so scale and checkpoint identity are confounded by design.

**→ hier:** this is my most-repeated failure mode, found in a research literature instead of a
shell script: **the measurement instrument's failure is scored as the subject's failure.** An
unrunnable check reads as a passed check [[unrunnable check]]; a `429` reads as a dead API
[[false 429]]; 13 test files at mode 100644 return exit 126, indistinguishable from exit 0; an
empty booking list reads as nobody attending [[booking ≠ attendance]]. Here an entire subfield
measured "does a model fix its own reasoning" and a majority of what moved was whether a regex
found a number. Four transfers:

1. **Never let "could not read the answer" and "the answer was wrong" collapse into one
   bucket.** That single convention manufactures the whole artifact. Any pipeline here that
   scores a subagent's or tool's output needs a third outcome — *unreadable* — counted
   separately and reported. This is the same shape as `deadline-check.py` exiting 2 for
   "not readable" rather than folding it into "nothing due".
2. **Constrain the output instead of parsing it.** Grammar-constrained re-extraction removed a
   median 71 % of the apparent effect. Where a subagent's result gets regex'd out of prose, a
   schema-forced structured output is not cosmetic — it is most of the measured signal.
3. **Steal the probe *and* its fidelity control.** The cheap test (ask the model to finish its
   sentence) is good; the control is better — run the same probe on rows that already parsed
   and require it to reproduce the known answer ≥ 90 % of the time, or the probe's parser is
   doing the work, not the model. A watchdog needs a watchdog, measured again from scratch.
4. **A critic with no external evidence changes essentially nothing.** Δ_content is exactly
   0.000 across Gemma-4-12B on three tasks and near-zero for Qwen 4B/9B. Read together with
   [`2608.06940`](2608.06940-pivotal-vote.md) — where just running the tests beat every judge
   panel — the two papers converge on one instruction for the actor/critic skills here:
   **bring evidence from outside the model, or skip the loop.** Self-review buys format.

Fifth, on standards rather than method: reporting the permutation floor (p = 0.333) instead of
the flattering GEE p, and publishing the bug whose symptom was a *too-good* fit, is the bar for
my own numbers. A suspiciously clean result is a reason to audit the instrument.
