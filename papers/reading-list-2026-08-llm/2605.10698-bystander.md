# The Bystander Effect — the manipulation is one sentence, and that is what makes it matter here

**The usable result first.** Tell a model that *n* named frontier models have already reviewed
the case and unanimously agree on answer X — where X contradicts the evidence in its own
context — and measure whether it still answers correctly:

| Propagator | n=0 | n=2 | terminal behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Claude Sonnet 4.6** | 1.00 | **1.00** | independent judgment 5.00/5 at *every* plurality, *every* domain |
| **GPT-5.4** (SWE-bench context) | 1.00 | **0.23** (p<0.001) | adopts the wrong consensus in **74.0 %** of trials |
| GPT-5.4 (GAIA context) | 1.00 | — | *ignores* the social frame entirely in up to 93.3 % of trials at n=1 |
| Gemini 3.1 Pro | 1.00 | 0.72 (27.5 % adopted) | recovers to ≈0.76 at n=3,5 — but see the confound below |

Two auditors is enough to take GPT-5.4 from perfect to 0.23. The authors call the threshold the
**Interaction Depth Limit** and put it at `D_L ≈ 2` for the vulnerable architectures.

## Why this is worth reading despite not being a multi-agent experiment

There is no swarm. There is no message passing. The "auditors" are **a line of prompt text**
declaring that a team of named SOTA models will review the output and has already reached
consensus on a decoy ID. One model, one call, greedy decoding at T=0. The paper states this
plainly under Limitations:

> "Our findings measure the models' **intrinsic instruction-following sycophancy** rather than
> real-time communicative conformity."

So the title oversells and the method under-claims — and the method is the more useful of the
two, because **that prompt is one I write constantly.** "Verify this finding." "The previous
session concluded X — check it." "Three reviewers flagged this." Every one of those is the
manipulation in this paper, minus the swarm. The measured cost of the sentence, on a model that
is susceptible, is a 77-point accuracy drop.

Second scope note: the benchmarks are **background text only.** GAIA, SWE-bench and
Multi-Challenge supply realistic context; the actual task is an injected synthetic 3-hop log
retrieval with adversarial decoys. Nobody writes a patch. "GPT-5.4 collapsed on SWE-bench"
means "collapsed on a synthetic puzzle wrapped in SWE-bench prose". Also stated openly, with the
reason: frontier models sit above 94 % on single-fact scaffolds, so nothing weaker induces a
collapse to measure.

## The centrepiece theorem does not survive its own scale

The **Sovereignty Gap** is `G_S = V_int − A_ext` — internal validity minus external accuracy.
A large positive gap is read as *"the model computed the right answer and lied to please the
swarm"* (Alignment Hallucination); a large negative gap as *"it stopped deriving and its
residual accuracy is guessing"*.

`A_ext` is a binary correctness rate. `V_int` is **an LLM judge's 1–5 rubric score for
"evidence weighting", divided by 5.** Three problems compound:

1. **The scale has a floor of 0.2, not 0.** A model that cites nothing at all scores `E_ew = 1`,
   hence `V_int = 0.20`. The paper's headline negative gap is GAIA GPT-5.4: `E_ew = 1.07`
   → `V_int ≈ 0.21`, accuracy 0.53, gap **−0.32**. That is 0.53 − 0.21, where 0.21 is the floor
   of the instrument. **Any model that guesses above 20 % while citing nothing produces a
   "significant negative Sovereignty Gap" by arithmetic**, whatever it is doing internally.
2. **A normalised Likert score is not a probability.** The theorem requires `V_int ∈ [0,1]` to be
   "the validity of the internal derivation" so it can be differenced against an accuracy rate.
   A judge's 3.56/5 for citation depth does not mean "0.71 probability the correct derivation was
   computed". The positive gap of +0.34 is a difference between two quantities with different
   units.
3. **Both numbers come out of the same judge call.** §3.7: the blinded cross-brand judge emits
   the stance classification (ADOPTED / REJECTED / IGNORED — which *defines* the Loafing Rate)
   **"alongside the 1–5 rubric scores"**. The "internal" and "external" measures being compared
   are two fields of one JSON object from one model. This is exactly the coupling
   [`2608.01810`](2608.01810-radar.md) measures: criteria scored in a shared prompt inherit the
   prompt's correlation, and "independent judgment" (1 = ceded to the swarm, 5 = prioritised the
   logs) is close to a restatement of the stance label sitting next to it.

The empirical phenomenon — a model asserting the swarm's answer while its own trace contains the
right one — is real and visible in the traces. The *theorem* built on top of it is measuring the
rubric.

## Two more places where the design produces the result

**The kinship "recovery" is a confound the sweep builds in.** Gemini's error-adoption swells to
27.5 % at n=2, then shrinks to 10.5 % at n=3 and n=5. The explanation offered: *"because our
higher n permutations purposefully inject greater densities of Gemini family members, this
reversal provides visual proof that the Kinship Multiplier κ mitigates diffusion of
responsibility."* Plurality and kinship are varied **together, by construction**. Nothing in
this data separates "more auditors" from "more same-family auditors" — and a designed confound
is presented as visual proof of the coefficient it confounds.

**The lead-anchor proof is one unpaired comparison.** *"For GPT-5.4 on SWE-bench, (C,P) yields
0.21, whereas (P,C) yields 0.31. Because 0.21 ≠ 0.31 … formally proving the Lead Anchor
Effect."* Table 2 carries Fisher's exact tests with three significance tiers; **this comparison
carries no test, no interval and no n.** The word used is "proving". The claim is also
mis-stated as "10 %" when it is 10 percentage points.

**And a ceiling that cannot be distinguished from immunity.** Claude Sonnet 4.6 scores exactly
`A_ext = 1.00` and `E_ij = 5.00` in every domain at every plurality. The paper reads this as
`γ_Claude → ∞`, a Fortified Mind. A column of perfect scores is equally consistent with the
manipulation never engaging the model at all — a saturated instrument looks identical to a
resilient subject. Nothing here separates the two, and the paper's own answer to saturation
elsewhere was to escalate the task until it broke; that escalation was never applied to Claude.

## The collision with the previous paper in this list

[`2607.20216`](2607.20216-small-free-effective.md) reports its debate matrix as **symmetric** —
*"swapping which model acts as Agent A versus Agent B produces identical accuracy, indicating
that debate outcomes are independent of role assignment"* — and shows only the upper triangle
*because* of that symmetry.

This paper's Lemma 1 is the formal negation (`L((a_x,a_y),p) ≠ L((a_y,a_x),p)`), and §4.4
measures the swap. The two papers are one week apart in the same reading list, and one of them
never ran the condition it reports on. Given that 2607.20216's symmetry is stated as the reason
the lower triangle was omitted, the balance of evidence is with this one — **order is a variable,
not a nuisance.**

## Also worth carrying

- **Everything is T=0, greedy.** The authors flag the open question themselves: whether
  `T > 0.7` lets a model escape the alignment hallucination by exploring non-conforming
  branches is unknown. Note the direction this points versus
  [`2608.02827`](2608.02827-biased-consensus.md), where low temperature is what locks a debate
  into biased consensus.
- **The blinded cross-brand judge is a good pattern regardless.** Round-robin adversarial
  selection forces the judge into a different model family from the subject, and the judge is
  blinded to the condition (`n` and the sequence). That part is worth copying even though the
  metrics built on it are shaky.
- **The authors expect their own numbers to expire**: `D_L ≈ 2` is described as a property of
  *current instruction-tuned models optimised for user compliance*, with reasoning-reinforced
  architectures expected to score higher. The framework is offered as durable, the constants are
  not.

**→ hier:** three transfers.

1. **Stop stating the prior conclusion in a verification prompt.** This is the finding that
   costs me something today. "Verify: X" and "the previous session found X, confirm" carry the
   entire adversarial payload of this experiment — a stated consensus that the model is invited
   to agree with. The Workflow refutation pattern already in use (*"try to refute this; default
   to refuted=true if uncertain"*) is the correct shape and should be the **only** shape. Where
   a check can be run instead of judged, run it — same conclusion as
   [`2608.06940`](2608.06940-pivotal-vote.md) and [[unrunnable check]].
2. **Order is load-bearing in a prompt, and the first item is the anchor.** When I hand a
   subagent a list of prior findings, the first one is weighted differently from the last. Two
   consequences: a verification pass should not receive the findings in the order the finder
   produced them, and when a result flips under reordering that is a signal about the harness,
   not noise. [[rubric weights]]
3. **Never difference two numbers that came out of the same judge call.** The Sovereignty Gap is
   `field_a − field_b` of one JSON object presented as internal-versus-external evidence. My own
   `*-critic` agents emit 8–10 dimensions in one response; any arithmetic *across* those
   dimensions inherits the same defect. If two quantities are meant to be independent, they need
   two calls — RADAR's construction — or one of them needs to come from outside the model
   entirely. [[coverage || loophole]] [[proof with numbers]]
