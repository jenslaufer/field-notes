# AgentMemBench — five memory strategies under one harness

Five families compared under identical conditions: **ICW** (sliding window of w recent turns),
**EKV** (dense embeddings in FAISS, top-k), **GEM** (spaCy-extracted entity graph, 2-hop),
**CBS** (LLM summarises every s turns + last 4 full turns), **WAM** (ICW plus web search when
query/window overlap < 0.15). 491 annotated question turns over LoCoMo (200), MultiDoc2Dial
(187), MSC (104). Generator *and* judge: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct in 4-bit, greedy — so the whole
pipeline is deterministic and a single seed reproduces everything.

## The one result worth remembering

**Long-range recall separates the strategies completely.** On LoCoMo, where the gold turn lies
many sessions back:

| | Recall@5 on LoCoMo |
|---|---|
| ICW | 0.000 |
| WAM | 0.000 |
| GEM | 0.001 |
| CBS | 0.005 |
| **EKV** | **0.573** |

Not a margin — a qualitative gap. The mechanism is structural and stated plainly: a recency
window has evicted the turn by definition; summarisation compresses away the specific evidence;
off-the-shelf NER on informal dialogue misses the entities that would bridge sessions. Only
dense retrieval indexes every turn at full resolution and retrieves independently of recency.

Macro-averaged over all three datasets: EKV Recall@5 **0.792**, CBS 0.556, ICW 0.468, WAM
0.462, GEM 0.361. Answer F1 is compressed (0.127–0.156) and faithfulness low in absolute terms
(0.219–0.354) — both bounded by the 7B generator, so read them as a *ranking*, not as levels.

## Where the paper's own headline breaks

The abstract says "EKV dominates on every quality axis" and prices its footprint at ~5,100 vs
~300 tokens for ICW. But Table 10, in §6.5, evaluates two published external systems on the
same harness:

| System | Recall@5 | MRR | F1 | Faith. | **Footprint** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EKV (theirs) | 0.792 | 0.677 | 0.156 | 0.354 | **5126** |
| **MemGPT/Letta** | **0.783** | **0.677** | **0.156** | 0.336 | **178** |
| HippoRAG | 0.706 | 0.612 | 0.143 | 0.266 | 5135 |

**A bounded FIFO main context plus FAISS archival recall matches EKV to within 0.009 Recall@5
and ties it on MRR and F1 — at 3.5 % of the token footprint.** The paper's own cost analysis
(Table 9: $76.90/day for EKV vs $3.00 for WAM at 100k turns) is therefore extrapolated from
the wrong operating point. The accuracy–efficiency trade-off the paper builds its discussion
around is largely dissolved by its own baseline table. Nothing in the text draws that
conclusion.

Two further honesty notes the author does make himself, both real:

- **WAM cannot beat ICW by construction.** Web results carry no in-corpus session provenance,
  so they can never count as a gold-session hit. WAM is "ICW with optional external
  grounding", not a recall strategy — and the paper's own recall numbers for it are therefore
  not measuring anything.
- **MADS, the proposed selector, picks the wrong strategy on all three datasets** (EKV on
  LoCoMo, ICW on the other two; the empirically best is EKV everywhere). Reported as a
  limitation rather than hidden, which is to the author's credit, but it means the heuristic
  contribution is unvalidated.
- Generator and judge share weights — a known leniency bias, conceded.

**→ hier:** this is the benchmark that measures the assistant's own memory design and rates it
last. `state/memory/` is windows (today + yesterday) plus summaries (`MEMORY.md`, the daily
notes) — ICW and CBS, the two strategies at 0.000 and 0.005 on genuine long-range recall.
The honest reading is not "build a vector store": it is that **a summary must not be trusted
to carry something six weeks back**, and that the recall path here is the one thing the
benchmark never measures — `grep` over `learnings.md`, which is exact where dense retrieval is
probabilistic. The MemGPT row is the design worth copying if this ever gets built: small
bounded context, everything else on disk, fetched on demand.
