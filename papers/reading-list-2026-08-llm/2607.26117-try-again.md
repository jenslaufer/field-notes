# Try again, don't look back — the control decides the result

Self-repair (hand the failed program back with its test output, ask for a fix) is normally
evaluated against a baseline that **does not retry at all**. That comparison mixes two things:
the value of the feedback and the value of the extra attempt. This paper runs the placebo.

Four matched-budget conditions on MBPP+ (378 tasks), Qwen2.5-Coder 1.5B/3B/7B, retries at
temperature 0.8, `k=8`, McNemar + Holm, everything pre-registered:

- **resample** — nothing appended. The extra draw alone.
- **placebo** — the failed program + a content-free "that was wrong". Isolates *self-exposure*.
- **feedback** — the failed program + the real test output. Isolates *diagnostic content*.
- **reflect** — feedback, preceded by "diagnose in prose first".

## The result

| Model | Condition | pass@1 (k=8) | Output tokens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1.5B** | resample | **0.749** | **63k** |
| | placebo | 0.688 | 90k |
| | feedback | 0.688 | 129k |
| | reflect | 0.688 | 345k |
| **3B** | resample | **0.778** | **76k** |
| | placebo | 0.709 | 89k |
| | feedback | 0.735 | 84k |
| | reflect | 0.743 | 284k |
| **7B** | resample | 0.820 | **49k** |
| | placebo | 0.802 | 59k |
| | feedback | 0.810 | 55k |
| | reflect | **0.828** | 122k |

Two pre-registered hypotheses about feedback are refuted:

- **Diagnostic content adds nothing measurable.** `feedback − placebo` = **+0.000, +0.026,
  +0.008** across scale, none significant. The test output — the part practitioners believe is
  the active ingredient — is indistinguishable from being told "wrong" with no detail.
- **Looking at your own failed attempt is harmful, not neutral.** `placebo − resample` =
  **−0.061 (p=0.006)** at 1.5B and **−0.069 (p<0.001)** at 3B. At 7B it closes to a tie.

## The mechanism: anchoring, measured directly

Textual similarity between consecutive attempts rises by **0.30–0.38** under every own-output
condition, and the share of retries that are near-identical (>0.95) goes from **2–14 %** to
**33–68 %**. Shown its own code the model edits; asked afresh it reconsiders.

That also explains the scale trend, which the original hypothesis did not predict. Anchoring
*strength* is roughly constant across scale; its *cost* falls, because a bigger model's failed
first attempt is a better thing to be stuck on. **7B is the point where anchoring becomes
cheap, not the point where feedback becomes informative** — the feedback-vs-placebo contrast is
null throughout.

Pooled over six configurations (two families, two precisions), the penalty is predicted by
**baseline quality alone, r = +0.96**:

| Model | Family | baseline pass@1 | placebo − resample |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.3B | DeepSeek | 0.201 | −0.138 |
| 1.5B FP16 | Qwen | 0.561 | −0.079 |
| 1.5B Q4 | Qwen | 0.566 | −0.061 |
| 3B | Qwen | 0.598 | −0.069 |
| 6.7B | DeepSeek | 0.608 | −0.045 |
| 7B | Qwen | 0.709 | −0.019 |

Two comparisons in that table do the work: Qwen-3B and DeepSeek-6.7B have nearly the same
baseline (0.598 / 0.608) and nearly the same penalty across *different* families — so family
explains little; DeepSeek-1.3B and 6.7B share a family and differ **3.1×** in penalty — so
capability explains much. Quantization is excluded separately: at FP16 the penalty does not
shrink, it grows slightly (−0.079 vs −0.061), and the anchoring measure is identical to within
0.003.

## Two controls that make the paper worth more than its headline

**The null that localizes the harm.** The obvious objection is that small models are simply
distracted by long prompts. So: prepend two *solved* exemplars from other tasks, random or
BM25-retrieved, leave-one-out. **Clean null, bounded to ±3.5 points**, all nine contrasts
non-significant. Same quantity of extra code in the context — a model's own failed attempt
costs 6.1 points, other tasks' successful solutions cost nothing. The harm is specific to
**self-conditioning**, which is what anchoring predicts and what "context dilution" does not.

**Four instrumentation faults caught before they reached a result**, each of which would have
produced a plausible wrong number: code extraction stripping leading indentation; run manifests
scored for an unavailable model; **a token budget that bound one arm and not another, so a raw
pass-rate gap measured budget fit rather than capability**; and greedy retries returning
byte-identical programs, which would have made `resample` a no-op and flattened every curve *by
construction*.

## Where the paper's own numbers undercut its presentation

**1. The cost headline is computed against the single most expensive arm.** "2.5–5.5× fewer
tokens" is exactly the `reflect` column (5.5× at 1.5B, 2.5× at 7B). Against the setup people
actually run — plain `feedback`, no reflection prose — resampling saves **2.05× at 1.5B but
only 1.11× at 3B and 1.12× at 7B**. The accuracy result stands on its own; the cost result
mostly measures how expensive it is to make a model think out loud.

**2. Table 1's caption is false at 7B.** It reads "The strongest condition is also the cheapest
at every scale" — but at 7B the strongest is `reflect` (0.828, 122k tokens), the *most*
expensive. §4 puts it correctly ("statistically indistinguishable from the best condition at
7B"); the caption, which is the quotable part, overreaches. Same shape as ChronoMem and Context
Codec: the sentence that travels is the flattering one.

**3. §7's "correspondingly the strongest of the three" is true at 3B and 7B only.** At 1.5B all
three own-output conditions are **exactly tied at 0.688** — §5 says so plainly ("no ordering
exists to recover"), §7 does not. §5 is otherwise unusually honest about the limits of its own
anchoring metric: placebo and feedback differ in anchoring by 0.003–0.039, inside the noise,
and their accuracy ordering does not follow.

**4. Retrieval is worse than random at 7B** (0.696 vs 0.728, n.s.) — reported without comment
in Table 2 while the section is titled "no effect".

Stated scope: one benchmark family, function-level synthesis, lexical retrieval, a single
reflection prompt, and — the one that matters most for transferring this — **nothing above 7B**.
The author names the sharpest remaining test himself: a model whose first attempts are good
enough that anchoring should cost nothing, which the correlation places "not far beyond the 7B
point".

**→ hier:** four transfers, and one refusal.

1. **The control determines the conclusion — this is now the third paper in a row saying it.**
   Measured against no-repair, the feedback condition *gains 12.2 points* at 1.5B; measured
   against blind resampling at matched budget, it loses. The treatment never changed, only the
   comparison. Pivotal Vote's permutation control and Calibration Floor's grammar-constrained
   control are the same instrument. **Before believing any critic/repair loop here helped: run
   the actor twice at the same token budget and compare against that**, not against one shot.
2. **When a subagent comes back wrong, re-dispatch fresh — do not paste its output back and ask
   for a fix.** 33–68 % near-identical regeneration is the measured cost of showing a model its
   own miss. This is the mechanical version of [[retraction hits generator]]: the withdrawn
   thing survives because it is still in the context.
3. **Cap the loop at two.** 46–53 % of all attainable gain lands by `k=2`, marginal returns are
   ~0 by `k=7`. Any of my "iterate until it's good" passes past the second round is spending
   tokens on noise.
4. **A budget that binds one arm and not the other measures the budget.** That fault is one line
   from my own recurring failure ([[unrunnable check]], [[false 429]]) and it is the reason the
   A/B comparisons in `topic_fabrik_output_quality` need their token ceilings checked before
   their outputs.

**The refusal:** this does **not** license "self-repair is useless" for Opus-class work here.
The penalty scales with baseline quality, and every model tested is ≤7B with a baseline pass@1
of 0.20–0.71. Extrapolating the r=0.96 line past 7B is the author's *proposed next experiment*,
not his result. What transfers unconditionally is the **method** — the matched-budget placebo —
not the sign of the effect.
