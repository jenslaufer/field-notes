# The best-disciplined paper in this reading list, and it publishes its own two losses

The pipeline: a small local model translates the problem into typed finite-domain facts, Horn-style
rules and constraints; a symbolic layer checks that every extracted fact is **traceable to a source
span**; a deterministic solver does the reasoning; and a repair loop fires only when diagnostics
localise a formalization failure. Everything runs on one consumer laptop through LM Studio —
Qwen3-4B primary, Phi-4-mini-reasoning and Gemma-3n-E4B as robustness checks.

The headline is real: on a 120-instance BBH-derived logical-deduction set, **0.933 against 0.283
for self-consistency at k=5**, one model call instead of five, McNemar p = 1.4 × 10⁻²¹ (80
VFR-only correct against 2 SC-only). But the paper is written so that you cannot quote that
number without the three that qualify it, and it prints a table — Table 6 — whose entire job is to
separate supported claims from interpretations the matrix does not justify. I have not seen that
in the other twenty-one.

## The three qualifications the paper prints itself

**1. The flagship win costs *more* tokens, not fewer.** Table 3: self-consistency 618.6 total
tokens, VFR-LLM **702.9** — the "token reduction vs. SC" column reads **−13.6 %**. The formalization
is longer than five short sampled answers. What is saved is *calls* (5 → 1) and *serial latency*
(16.5 s → 11.3 s). Verbatim: *"The resource benefit here is reduced repeated sampling and latency,
not token reduction."* An entire abstract could have been built on "saves compute"; the author
declined and named the unit instead. [[proof with numbers]]

**2. Two of nine conditions are losses, and one is a rout.** The decision column of Table 5:

| condition | model | Direct | SC | VFR | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Precedence | Qwen | 0.675 | 0.700 | **0.983** | strong support |
| BBH pairwise | Qwen | 0.850 | 0.867 | **0.967** | strong support |
| BBH extended | Qwen | 0.317 | 0.283 | **0.933** | calls/latency only |
| Typed | Qwen | **0.850** | 0.833 | 0.817 | **blocked by direct** |
| Typed (CPU) | Qwen | **0.850** | 0.833 | 0.817 | **blocked by direct** |
| Typed | **Phi** | **0.183** | 0.133 | **0.042** | **negative** |
| Precedence | Gemma | 0.375 | 0.367 | **0.683** | support vs SC |
| Typed | Gemma | 0.408 | 0.433 | **0.608** | mixed |
| BBH extended | Gemma | 0.350 | 0.350 | 0.375 | marginal (p = 0.72) |

The Phi row is the one to keep. **A formalization pipeline running on a model that cannot
formalize is four times worse than just asking the model** (0.042 against 0.183) — and slower, and
it looks rigorous the whole time.

**3. Where the model is already good, the machinery is pure cost.** Qwen "typed": direct answering
is *more accurate and cheaper* than the full pipeline. The paper gives this its own verdict label —
**"blocked by direct"** — which is a better concept than anything I had for it. Third independent
arrival at the same place: Pivotal Vote's "just run the tests" beat every panel, Wix decided
executability before the model saw the menu, and here the plain call wins outright in two of nine
cells.

## The ablation that changes how I build critics

Table 10, on the same Qwen BBH-extended runs:

| variant | accuracy | traceability accept | abstention |
|---|---|---|---|
| basic solver (no checks) | 0.275 | — | 0.000 |
| **traceability filter, no repair** | **0.250** | 0.258 | **0.742** |
| full VFR (check **and** repair) | **0.933** | 0.942 | 0.000 |

**The verification layer alone scores below doing nothing.** It accepts 25.8 % of formalizations and
is right on 25.0 % — so its precision on what it accepts is ~97 %, an excellent filter — and it
throws away three quarters of the work. All of the gain lives in the **repair** step (3.2 repairs
per instance, Table 3).

Two things follow, and they pull in opposite directions, which is why this is the most useful
table in the paper:

- **A critic that can only reject is worse than no critic.** It is precise and it is destructive.
  Every one of my `*-critic` agents is exactly this shape today: it scores, it gates, and there is
  no defined repair channel back into the actor beyond "try again". This is the same finding as
  Try Again's placebo control and Calibration Floor's evidence-free critic, but stronger — those
  measured *no effect*, this measures a *loss*.
- **And the 0.250 is itself an accounting choice the paper discloses**: abstentions are counted as
  incorrect. That is precisely the conflation Calibration Floor spent a paper on — unreadable
  scored as wrong. Read the other way, the filter answers a quarter of the time at 97 % precision,
  which is a perfectly good abstaining classifier. The number is honest because the caption says
  which convention produced it. [[unrunnable check]]

## The bottleneck is translation, never inference — measured cleanly

Table 7, deterministic ablation with no model in the loop at all:

| method | accuracy |
|---|---|
| text-order heuristic | 0.067 |
| precedence-only solver | 0.208 |
| **typed finite-domain solver** | **1.000** |

The solver solves **every** instance when handed the right formalism. So nothing that goes wrong in
the real-model runs is a reasoning failure; all of it is translation. PrologMCP claimed this;
this paper is the one that measured the ceiling.

And the model split has a **cheap leading indicator**: automatic full-traceability rate 0.942 for
Qwen → 0.933 accuracy; 0.292 for Gemma → 0.375 accuracy. You can tell whether the pipeline will
work for a given model *before* running the benchmark, by measuring whether its formalizations are
grounded in source spans. That is a pre-flight test in the same family as PhantomFill's "measure
EUR for your model before trusting the escape" and the Co-Failure Ceiling's β — **all three say:
price the model against the harness before wiring it in.** [[co-failure ceiling]]

## What transfers

1. **Never ship a check without a repair path.** Table 10 is the number. If `/quality-gate` or a
   critic can only say no, the expected value of adding it is negative once you count the discarded
   work. Repair beat verification 0.933 to 0.250 on identical inputs.
2. **"Blocked by direct" belongs in my vocabulary.** Before building any pipeline: does the plain
   call already win? Two of nine cells here, and the pipeline was slower in both.
3. **A pipeline amplifies a model's weakness, it does not compensate for it.** Phi 0.183 → 0.042.
   The `simple` alias (qwen3:8b) is the local instance of this: it is the model class this paper
   tests, and one of its three models is destroyed by the harness. Measure the traceability
   analogue first; do not assume structure rescues a weak model. [[co-failure ceiling]]
4. **Name the resource you saved.** Calls, latency, tokens and money are four different claims, and
   this paper's flagship result wins three of them and loses one. Mine should say which.

## Craftsmanship note

Best in this reading list, and it sets the bar I want for my own notes. An abstract that carries
its own negative results ("Phi is negative on typed constraints", "The contribution is bounded").
A table (6) devoted to what the evidence does *not* support. Deterministic negative controls
(Table 9) where the solver must return unsat rather than a plausible order. A disjoint 60-instance
heldout set. And a limitation that polices the author's own strongest result for a leak:
*"A deterministic repair that uses the source text is acceptable only if the transformation is
specified before the experiment, logged, and auditable. Otherwise, it could become an implicit
post-hoc reasoning channel."* Followed by the admission that the shipped audit packet is
infrastructure, **not** an independent blinded human audit.

The sentence I want to keep from the limitations, because it is the risk of everything in section
4: *"If the selected rule-and-constraint language cannot express the relevant semantics, the
verification layer may produce a false sense of rigor."*
