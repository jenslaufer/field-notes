# Let a personal agent mine Facebook groups, then sell the match

> Meta Muse can search public Facebook groups, Marketplace and Instagram. Greg's play: pick one
> service in one city, let Muse find supply and demand, clean it into your own list, and take a
> fee for each match.

**Source note:** a 24-minute solo screen share with one live demo (night nurses in Montreal).
No revenue from anyone. "A thousand plus millionaires" is a forecast, not a measurement.

## Insights

1. **The trigger was his own search.** He looked for a night nurse after his baby was born. The
   agency admitted *"they were going through Facebook groups"* for Miami, South Florida or New
   York night nurses — and charged a fee for it.
2. **The model: one niche × one city.** *"night nurse in Tampa, furniture in Austin, plumbers in
   Phoenix"*. Muse watches the groups and does *"the structure, the matching, the drafting DMs,
   the calls and texts and booking"*.
3. **The asset is the cleaned list, not the search.** Posts are *"dirty data"*. Muse filters them
   into Airtable or a Google Sheet: names, needs, prices, trust signals. *"you own that asset"*
   — and he hints the list itself could be sold later (data brokering, a promised future episode).
4. **Muse does not mine groups by default — you have to tell it.** His first query came back from
   public listing sites (CanadianNanny.ca) and an Instagram sweep, *"there were no groups"*. Only
   after he asked for 20 night nurses from Facebook groups did it pull group posts (Montreal moms,
   West Island community, Filipino caregiver Montreal). His read: *"Zuck is smart. He's not going
   to, by default, cannibalize Marketplace and Instagram."* Private groups need you to join them.
5. **A phone number for the agent.** People connect Muse to Bland AI's agent phone plan
   ("AgentLine", ~$15 by his memory) so it can call and text.
6. **Pricing idea:** a success fee of *"a few hundred dollars"* per match. An agency with five
   people cost thousands; the agent version can be cheaper and still high-margin.
7. **Marketplace tactics (physical goods):** mispriced or mislabeled listings, bad photos,
   long-sitting items, pickup-only items to bundle and ship (Mac minis), price drops and
   relistings (sellers more willing to deal), and let Muse negotiate. He prefers services.
8. **Four levels.** (1) mining other people's groups, (2) owning the cleaned list, (3) owning the
   room — your own Facebook group, niche IG page, Marketplace storefront, (4) building the
   software that automates the workflow end to end (vertical agent / harness).
9. **Facebook groups and pages as an asset class.** If millions of agents will crawl groups,
   *"why aren't you creating a Facebook group right now?"* He calls groups, business pages and IG
   niche pages "undervalued" and mentions buying groups — with his own caveat to check Meta's
   terms of service.
10. **Plans:** free, $100 and maybe $200 a month; he pays $100 for the better model
    ("Muse Wonderful" instead of "Spark") and more tokens. Start free, upgrade once clients pay.

## What it means for Jens

- **Legal wall in Germany.** Level 2 — a stored list of named private persons with needs, prices
  and trust signals, possibly resold — is processing personal data without consent. Under the
  DSGVO that is the risky half of the idea; Greg does not mention privacy law at all.
- **No network needed — that is the part that fits.** The model starts from public demand, not
  from contacts, and Jens has no network to start from. The same move already exists in
  his own setup: freelancermap postings are mined for matches every night.
- **Not checked here:** whether Muse is available in Germany and whether it searches German
  groups. Measure that before any plan.
