# A GrokBot content pipeline: research, plan, script, edit, publish from the phone

> Louise runs her short-form and YouTube content through GrokBot: weekly research routines,
> a group chat of two bots, advisor skills, and an MCP link into her video editor. Her aim:
> *"automate 80% of my process"* and keep the time for script writing and craft.

**Source note:** the guest works at Tella and edits everything in Tella; she says so. Her
results are unmeasured self-reports: *"I gained 6,500 followers in the past three months"*
on Instagram, one YouTube video at *"50,000 views"*. Greg's *"hundreds of thousands of views"*
in two weeks, also unmeasured. Her diagrams were lost before recording. The ASR spells GrokBot
as "Growpot", "Grog", "Gawk", "Brock"; Tella as "Tela", "Stella", "Tala".

**What is new vs. the earlier GrokBot notes:** Nick (11-02) sells GrokBot clones to SMBs; Billy
(08-21) runs a newsletter on an agent team. This one is a concrete content-creator stack —
research routines, a bot group chat, mentor skills and on-the-go video editing via MCP.

## Research routines (mostly weekly)

She runs them weekly *"because of usage"*: daily, *"I'll run out of Gawk usage very quickly."*

1. **Outlier posts.** A Notion list of creators she saves while scrolling (*"save this
   creator"*). Once a week the bot checks their profiles for outlier posts and saves topic,
   hook and how much better it did to a second database. Start with *"15, 20, 25 creators"*
   — brands and publications count. Pick them yourself: when AI picks, *"it diverges so far
   from who I actually want to be like or imitate."*
2. **Hook library.** She saves TikTok videos to a "hooks" collection. Weekly, the bot extracts
   the first two sentences of each and files them in a database, organized by type
   (contrarian, questions, relatable). Hooks from other niches transfer: *"even B2B buyers are
   humans."*
3. **Trend digest.** Reads her paid Substack newsletters in the inbox, X and news outlets
   against a defined niche. Signals become trends as more stories cover them (example: MCPs as
   a user acquisition channel). Built from a friend's instructions; she added sources.
4. **Rising keyword.** Weekly scan in vidIQ for keywords rising in search volume, as YouTube
   video ideas. Even a keyword she would not cover sparks a related idea.
5. **Capture on the go.** Send any link via phone chat; the bot files it in Notion.

## Planning

- Every Monday she plans with "Production Polly", her content bot, in a Notion calendar view.
- Connect Google Calendar and let the bot block time for recording, scripting, editing. She
  tried it: *"I'm going to sit and script write for four hours and then I'll have my script
  for the week."*

## Scripting: group chat plus advisor skills

1. **Group chat of bots.** Production Polly (knows her writing and hooks) talks to "Trend
   Study" (*"more like a journalist"*). Polly combines her saved ideas with a rising trend; the
   run she showed produced three content ideas. Instruction kept simple: *"you guys need to
   collaborate on content because this is what you both bring."* Greg: group chats are *"a
   highly underrated feature"*; most setups keep bots siloed.
2. **Idea critic skill** — tells her directly whether an idea is *"not that deep"*.
3. **Hook advisor** — has access to all saved hooks plus her own hook templates (past vs.
   future; *"here are three things to avoid"*).
4. **Script advisor** — her scripts follow *"a very set structure."*
5. **Mentor skill.** Every piece of feedback from her course mentor Miranda goes into a skill
   called "Miranda feedback"; she runs it on each new script. Greg's extension: several
   mentors as an advisory board.
6. Feed skills examples and your best posts, and analyze *"why it did the best"*.

**Bot vs. skill.** No strict rules. One bot per area of work (*"maybe like a variety of four
to five things that you do"*), plus private bots (she has a ceramics bot). A skill is a
repeated method: *"Once you have a method to your madness, then it's like, skillify it."*
Also make skills for basic tasks — her Notion-saving skill exists because the bot otherwise
*"saves it in like with way too much text."*

## Editing via MCP

1. From the phone, tell the bot to create a new Tella project.
2. While researching, send tweets, screenshots, Slack messages, Figma frames: *"add all of this
   to my media library"*. B-roll is waiting when she sits down to edit.
3. After recording, on a walk: remove silences, filler words, repetitions by the time she is
   home.
4. In the editor: ask for motion graphics, or *"edit like this video"*.
5. Alternatives without Tella: Claude or Codex, editing locally. Her earlier viral post was
   about clipping long-form with Claude instead of Opus Clips.

## Publishing

- The YouTube bot reads the transcript via MCP, checks rising keywords in vidIQ (a skill) and
  proposes titles. Then it writes the description with chapters and timestamps. Tella posts
  to YouTube or Shorts directly.
- Her Remotion video (*"People are talking about it, but are they making videos about it?"*)
  followed this rising-keyword rule.

## Model choice

- Louise: Opus 5.5 on medium; *"Fable 5.2 is super expensive"*, keep it for complex 3D work
  like Blender. A cheaper model only works with *"super clear references"* and few motion
  graphics.
- Greg runs everything on high: *"even if it costs me an extra 20% or 15%, I don't know the
  exact math"*.

## Greg's frame

- *"Media operates in formats. And your goal is to figure out formats and then repeat those
  formats."*
- Owning top-of-funnel search terms (*"how to do XYZ"*) is a reason to make video now.

## What it means for Jens

- **The machine assumes a face and an audience; Jens has neither as a channel today.** Her
  numbers are follower growth on a personal account. Copy the parts that work without one.
- **The rising-keyword rule is SEO.** Greg's point — own the top-of-funnel *"how to do XYZ"*
  searches — is the calculator sites' job, where authority, not ideas, is the bottleneck. A
  weekly rising-keyword scan feeds topics; it does not fix authority.
- **The mentor skill is what the feedback memories and the `klartext` gate already do:**
  every correction collected in one place and run on each new draft. Her group chat of bots
  is the part to skip — one strong agent per task holds here.
