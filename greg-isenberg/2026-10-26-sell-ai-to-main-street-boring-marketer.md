# Sell funnels, not AI, to $2–20M trades businesses

> Don't sell "AI agents" to local businesses. Find trades firms that already pay for ads and
> sell them a fix for the leak between paid click and booked job: a funnel built fast with a
> coding agent. Find them with a Dream-100 list, warm them up for months with free value,
> and get the first case study for free.

**Source note:** written from the publisher's VTT transcript. Every number is said aloud,
none verified. James sells his own tool (boringfunnels.com) along the way; he says "do it
however you want".

## Insights

1. **Customer: $2–20M trades businesses.** They can pay, they suspect they are behind, and
   *"really nobody useful is showing up"*. Solopreneurs and startups often can't pay. Picking
   the niche solves most "bad client" problems.
2. **Never say "AI".** Owners think "agent" means a real estate agent, and many dislike AI.
   His AI phone receptionist test got voicemails like *"is this a dumb AI thing?"* and
   hang-ups. Sell booked jobs and calls.
3. **The leak is the page, not the traffic.** He checked five Dallas plumbers rated 4.8–4.9
   who were running Google Ads: 4 of 5 didn't repeat the ad's promise on the landing page,
   3 of 5 didn't show their Google rating, and 4 of 5 put booking three screens down.
   Agencies sell ads and SEO; the conversion problem stays.
4. **High ticket and urgent only.** Plumbing, HVAC and restoration yes; lawn care and
   restaurants no. His cited figures: about 112,000 US plumbing/HVAC firms, about $2.5M
   average revenue, 14% of owners say AI is fully part of how they run, $129 per lead.
5. **The ROI math sells.** 100 leads × $129 = about $13,000/month. If the page loses 20%,
   that's about $2,600 wasted. One plumbing job (~$1,714) pays for 13 leads.
6. **Funnel types for local businesses:** free check; tripwire ($79 tune-up, which pays for
   the ad spend); instant price; quiz ("repair or replace?"); owner video for $20–30k
   installs; advertorial. Send each search intent to its own funnel. Most firms send all
   traffic to one page.
7. **Old-school direct response plus AI:** Chet Holmes' Dream 100 (2,200 advertisers, 167
   bought 95% of the ads; 28 clients in month six), Jay Abraham (be an advisor, not a
   vendor), Dan Kennedy (message-to-market fit, "lumpy mail"). AI does the research for each
   of the 100 (reviews, site, owner, Reddit language in that city).
8. **You don't need an audience.** One trade, one city, 25-mile radius, Meta ads aimed at
   that group. *"You don't even need to be the authority. You can just be on authority."* (ASR: "an authority")
9. **Branding should feel right to the customer, not to designers.** Stripe/Apple polish
   can miss Main Street.
10. **Personal agents (Muse etc.) aren't a lead source yet.** He gets a few ChatGPT visitors
    a week. Google Maps reviews and AI Mode are still where people look.
11. **Getting started:** build it free for a case study ("the cost to you is tokens and some
    of your time"), then charge setup plus a monthly fee, maybe performance pricing later.
    Run one market for six months.
