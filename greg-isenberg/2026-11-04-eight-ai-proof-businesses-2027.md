# Eight moats for when software, content and answers cost nothing

> Greg's map: physical work, permission, attention, timing, belonging, liquidity, unique data,
> outcomes. Physical and permission rank highest, paid-for-results lowest. Mix several.

**Source note:** a solo monologue, explicitly *"to get your creative juices flowing"*. No
numbers about any business he runs. The "hundreds of thousands of views" on his own short
videos is the only figure about himself, unmeasured.

## The eight categories

1. **Atoms.** Physical businesses — coffee shop, plumber: *"AI is not going to be able to
   fix that leak."* He argues their valuations rise because tech money will chase the moat.
2. **Permission.** Licenses, regulation, partnerships. A preferred-vendor deal with a
   medium or large company *"is a partnership that can't be copied on day one."* Where a
   license is required (law firm, bookkeeping in some countries): partner with the licensed
   person and run marketing, growth and AI yourself.
3. **Attention.** *"People follow people."* Build a media company, not one page —
   newsletters, meme pages, founder-led content around a micro niche. He now edits his
   short videos with an AI model and finally enjoys making them.
4. **Timing.** Monitor micro-niche trends with an agent, build first. The first product
   *"might be the least AI-proof"*; what is AI-proof is the data, brand and trust collected
   by being early.
5. **Belonging.** Paid communities, weekly accountability calls, IRL events. Example:
   Sam Parr's Hampton (meetups for founders above a revenue threshold) — *"What is the
   Hampton for XYZ"*. His own Nashville event had more demand than they could meet. The hard
   part: niche, goal, experience, a no-brainer price.
6. **Liquidity.** Marketplaces are among *"the most sticky businesses of all time."* Example:
   a friend's weekly curated auction for collectible cars, marketed through his Instagram
   audience. Partner with a creator who has attention, or grow the attention yourself. Agents
   now carry the support load, so a team of one to five can run one.
7. **Unique data.** Two forms: sell data the frontier labs lack (as Mercor and others did
   with coding data), or a **curated directory** people pay for — because LLMs and feeds
   give infinite content. His example: restaurant reviews that are not just five stars or
   one star. He flags consumer as riskier than enterprise, prosumer and SMB.
8. **Outcomes.** Automated AI services — a creative agency run by models, or a machine that
   bids on Fiverr/Upwork work. Moat = the best context, data and system. **He ranks this one
   last.**

## Close

Mix categories (attention + belonging, permission + liquidity, outcomes + unique data).
*"sometimes when there's the most amount of chaos, it creates the most amount of opportunity."*

## What it means for Jens

- **What he has built so far is mostly pixel software** — extensions, calculator sites,
  agent harnesses — the thing Greg says is now free to build; the house in Tannheim is
  atoms. The list is a menu for the next venture, not a verdict on the current ones.
- **The Fiverr idea he asked about on 23.10.** is Greg's category 8 — the one he ranks
  lowest. Its moat is the system and data, not the bidding machine.
- **Attention and belonging need an audience;** Jens has no network (see
  `insights-for-jens.md`). Timing and unique data are the two that start without one.
