# The war on agents: who blocks, who opens up, and nine stages

> Amazon blocks Meta's Muse, Delta works with no outside agents, Yelp lets them in only for
> a fee. Greg's model: an agent is *"a customer with a credit card and no eyes"*. The fight
> is only about the third step, payment. He expects a Plaid-like trust layer, then
> competition for agents, then ads aimed at agents, and finally "agent-buyable" becomes as
> normal as having a website.

**Source note:** written from the publisher's VTT transcript. The company facts (Amazon,
Delta, Yelp, Zillow, Walmart) are Greg's, none verified. **Stage six is missing** in the
transcript: he goes straight from five to seven, but later mentions "stage six" twice.

## Insights

1. **Three camps.** *Block:* Amazon (Muse, earlier Perplexity), Delta ("unauthorized
   automated activity"), Yelp (only via paid data licensing). *Block by accident:* Zillow
   (anti-scraping catches real agents), Walmart (Muse partner, failures "an accident").
   *Open:* Meta with Walmart/Stripe on an open standard, Shopify (agentic storefronts),
   Visa/Mastercard (recognising trusted agents, with Cloudflare).
2. **An agent is a customer with a credit card and no eyes.** It ignores your homepage
   video, banners and countdowns. It reads product data, price, shipping and the returns
   policy.
3. **Three levels of trust:** research → prepare (cart, hold, quote) → pay. Almost every
   company accepts the first. The fight is only over payment, yet many block all three.
4. **Agents are disciplined and loyal to what works.** They find the hidden shipping fee
   and compare five suppliers at once. They come back to whichever site worked last time,
   and if your checkout breaks, they leave without you noticing.
5. **Why companies block (follow the money):** fair reasons are wrong orders, fraud and
   scrapers posing as agents. The money reason: agents skip sponsored products (Amazon ads)
   and upsells (airline seats, bags, card offers). The deeper fear: Meta/OpenAI sitting
   between the store and its customer. The same companies build their own (Rufus, Delta's
   concierge).
6. **Greg revises his own rule.** "Own the customer relationship" can turn into *"make the
   customer deal with me the way I prefer"*. His test: *"Are you protecting the customer or are you
   protecting a habit you'd like the customer to keep?"*
7. **Who bets on what:** Amazon wants to be the everything-store with its own assistant;
   Meta opens the door because it owns the agents; Walmart, as number two, uses the new
   channel to take share; OpenAI wants checkout inside the chat; Google has to protect its
   click-based ads; Shopify sells to every side; Visa/Mastercard/Cloudflare want to be the
   toll booth.
8. **Historical pattern: Mint → Plaid.** Banks went from blocking to signing deals to
   charging for access.
9. **The nine stages:** (1) block, because agents skip ads and upsells — today; (2) arms
   race between workaround and new block; (3) split: sellers let agents in, data businesses
   charge, giants build their own; (4) a trusted middle layer for identity and payment
   becomes standard (the "Plaid moment"); (5) businesses that welcome agents win customers
   from those that block; (6) — missing; (7) everyone competes for agents (prices, checkout,
   product pages, agent SEO); (8) **ads come back**, paying to be the agent's first
   recommendation (ChatGPT is already testing this) — the stage he says few people see;
   (9) selling to agents is as normal as having a website.
10. **What a business should do this week:** take 10 real buying questions from support and
    give each to 2–3 agents to see where they get stuck; check whether bot protection blocks
    agents by accident; put price, delivery date, compatibility and returns in plain text
    (not in a PDF or on the last checkout screen); make your advantage readable too
    (delivery tomorrow vs. 3 weeks); set rules per level (research free, quote OK, extra
    check before payment).

## Business ideas

| # | Idea | Stage |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Agent readiness for one industry** (dental supply, spare parts): record 3 cases of an agent picking the wrong item on a supplier site, show what's missing, fix it. Do it by hand 10 times, then build the software | 5–7 |
| 2 | **Plaid for one vertical:** the trusted link between agents and the messy systems of one industry (contractors, restaurant supply, vet appointments) | 4 |
| 3 | **Insurance/guarantee for agent mistakes:** whoever pays for the wrong order opens the door for nervous businesses | 4 and 6 |
| 4 | **Analytics for agent traffic** ("Google Analytics for agents"): which agents visit, what they ask, why they leave | 5–7 |
| 5 | **Be the specialist:** a supplier excellent at one thing gets found by agents | 7–9 |
| — | **Avoid:** anything whose edge is a trick to get mentioned by AI. Tricks stop working | — |
