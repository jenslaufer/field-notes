# Eight repos and skills, each with a way to get paid

> The repo format from 02.09. again, because the audience voted for it. Five GitHub repos and
> three skills, recorded at night. Each one comes with one small first test and one way to turn
> it into a paid service. The common thread is not the tools: **do the work for someone before
> they ask, send it to them, and use it to start the conversation.** Greg repeats this pattern
> three times (launch video, design critique, Product Hunt).

**Source note:** written from the publisher's VTT transcript of the full episode. Names come from
the transcript and are given as heard: "Brag"/"BragSlim", "Agent Reach", "Voice Studio",
"PPT Master", "OpenRig", "Free Tools" (Corey Haynes), "Grill Me" (Matt Pocock), "Impeccable"
(Paul Bacasse). Check the real repo names before installing anything. Greg says he has *"no
affiliation"* with any of them. No numbers on results, stars or costs, apart from his
$1,000/month example.

## The eight

1. **Brag (BragSlim)** — a skill that makes a short launch video with motion and music, plus
   the copy for the post, from your project or a website link. Vertical, square, probably
   landscape; makes a thumbnail; you can redo single scenes. Greg says BragSlim is the version
   *"designed for Opus 5.5"*. First test: *"make a 20 second launch video for this"*.
   Money: go through Product Hunt launches **without** a video, make one, send it — *"instead
   of cold email, cold outreach, do things for people, send it to them"*. His pick if you try
   only one tonight.
2. **Agent Reach** — sets up the tools an agent needs to read YouTube, Reddit and X, so it can
   research a topic across sources. Use: customer research. Search phrases like *"I export it"*,
   *"I copy this into"*, *"does anyone know a workaround"* around software people already pay
   for. Example: cleaning-company owners who export schedules every Friday to work out pay — a
   small tool that takes the export and does the calculation, *"so they keep the software they
   know"*. Caveat he gives himself: *"You still need that conversation before assuming there's
   a customer here."* Second use: take the customers' own words for your copy.
3. **Voice Studio** — a desktop app for AI voice, *"like an open source 11 labs"*, local models.
   Speech, voice clone from your own recording, dubbing into another language. First test: one
   clean voice sample, a few lines, listen. Money: translate onboarding and how-to videos for
   customers in other languages, *"with a fluent person checking the result"*; start with the
   three support questions that keep coming up; sell upkeep as a monthly retainer because
   products change. Check the commercial licence; clone only voices you may use.
4. **PPT Master** — documents or notes → editable PowerPoint, with your own template. First
   test: a proposal you already wrote, so you know what good looks like. Then after a sales
   call: notes + scope + price → deck while the call is fresh. The rule worth keeping:
   *"Write the deck for the person who wasn't on the call."*
5. **OpenRig** — organises coding agents into a team: one builds, one checks, work is passed
   between them. Works with Codex and Claude Code. Needs Mac/Linux, Node, tmux; *"it changes
   some agent settings"* — read them first. Use when you are the one copying results between
   chat windows. Test: a quote calculator, one agent builds, the other checks it against the
   business's pricing.
6. **Free Tools** (Corey Haynes' marketing skill collection) — works out what free tool to build
   and give away to attract the right buyers. Example: a box calculator for a packaging
   supplier. Two design rules: *"I'd let them see the result before asking for their email"*,
   then turn the estimate into a quote without re-entering anything.
7. **Grill Me** (Matt Pocock) — interviews you about an idea or decision in rounds. Use it
   *before* building what you are most excited about, because that is when you skip the awkward
   question. Example: local ads for franchises — who pays, and does the payer have the right to
   approve the ad, or does head office? The instruction he adds: track every sentence with
   "probably" in it (*"they probably have budget"*) as an assumption, rank which to check first,
   and take it to a customer. *"The customer is the one who can tell you what's actually
   happening."*
8. **Impeccable** (Paul Bacasse) — design guidance for coding agents. Commands `critique`,
   `polish`, `bolder`, `quieter`. Start with `critique` on a page you already built, fix only
   what you agree with, focus on onboarding or checkout. Money: pick a small product whose site
   makes it hard to see what you buy, show the founder two or three specific changes, explain
   why each helps beyond taste — then offer more. His arithmetic: *"That's $1,000 a month, 10
   clients, $10,000 a month."*

## The pattern behind the list

- **Give first, then sell.** Launch video for a Product Hunt launch, design critique for a small
  product, translated tutorial for a SaaS. The finished artefact is the outreach.
- **Services first, technology later.** *"Every big business starts as a small business"*; you
  learn the niche through services and build the software from there.
- **Boring is the opportunity.** The spreadsheet step between paid software and payroll, the
  deck the sales rep rebuilds, the tutorial only in English.

## What it means for Jens

- **Three of the eight already exist in the setup under other names.** Grill Me ≈ the
  `brainstorming`/`spec` step before a build; Impeccable ≈ `webdesign-critic` and
  `design-review`; OpenRig ≈ actor/critic agents plus the quality gate before a merge. Nothing
  to install here.
- **Grill Me's one rule is worth copying anyway:** list every "probably" as an assumption and
  check the most expensive one with a customer first. It is the same rule as
  "validate before build" in the assistant — the skill only makes it a habit at the
  moment of excitement.
- **Brag is the cheapest test with a direct channel.** FinGrab's Chrome Web Store listing can
  carry a promo video; a 20-second clip from the website is one prompt. Measure: listing
  conversion before vs after, not views.
- **Free Tools fits FinGrab's model** (free extension, paid tier) and its rule *show the result
  before the email* fits the Launch Kit opt-ins.
- **Agent Reach's search phrases are the useful part,** not the tool. "I export it to Excel"
  is FinGrab's own buyer sentence; the phrases work in a normal search, too.
- **Discount:** no results, no prices, no failure cases. A list of things he tested at night.
