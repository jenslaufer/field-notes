# Niche sweep

A wide first pass when you have no niche, or too many. 4–50 niches, three data points each, then pick 2–3 for the [opportunity scan](04-opportunity-scan.md). The sweep is a filter: no market model, no candidates, no verdict.

Fewer than 4 niches → go straight to the scan.

## Before you start

1. **List the niches.** Phrase each as a job ("export Trello boards to Excel"), not a category ("project management"). No list? Derive one from:
   - your products and their numbers, your code, your domain knowledge;
   - store categories;
   - TrustMRR top products that are themselves store products (extension, plugin, app).
2. **Check the graveyard.** A match drops the niche.
3. **Note your effort ceiling** (weekend / medium / big). It passes to the scan.

## Per niche: three questions (15–20 min, ~15 page views)

Check each niche on its own. Do not let one niche's result colour the next: judge each from its sources alone.

### 1. Top 3 complaints

What do people with this job complain about? Look on Reddit, HN, forums and the 1★ reviews of store listings. Three distinct complaints, one verbatim quote each, with URL and date.

### 2. What they pay for today

Only **in-store payers** count: a product in an app store or marketplace (Chrome Web Store, Firefox Add-ons, Apify, Atlassian, WordPress.org, Shopify, Slack, VS Code, …) that charges for this job or a substitute.

Note per payer: name, store, price, users or ratings, its rank by users among the store's listings for the job, and whether it does the job itself or is a substitute. Look for a revenue figure of the store product too (TrustMRR, founder posts, marketplace sales).

- A SaaS price is context, never a payer.
- A vendor-wide revenue figure counts only when the store product is the vendor's only product.
- Nothing found → write `none found` and what you searched.

### 3. How hard to reach

Where do people with this job gather (subreddits with member counts, forums, newsletters)? Do they search the store for it (store autocomplete phrasing)?

- **easy** = a community with members plus store search phrasing
- **medium** = one of the two
- **hard** = neither

Tag every number `verified`, `unverified` or `suspicious`.

## Score

0–2 each, one evidence line per score.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain | no complaints found | complaints, but scattered or older than 2 years | three distinct, dated complaints |
| Payer | none passes the gate | a substitute passes | a product for this job passes |
| Reach | hard | medium | easy |

**Payer gate** (same as the scan's paid-proof gate): a paid listing passes on one of three paths — it ranks **top 5 by users** in its job; or it shows a public price plus **≥ 25 ratings** (stores without user counts); or a public revenue figure of **≥ $500/month** for the store product itself.

- A suspicious number never carries a 2.
- An unavailable number counts as not found.

## Decide

- Payer 0 → the niche never ranks. Park it: `park: no payer found` or `park: tiny payer`, with what you searched.
- Rank the rest by total /6; ties go to the stronger payer.
- Where two niches disagree on the same product (different price, one calls it free), recheck once. Still split → mark the number suspicious and note both values.
- Pick 2–3 from the top 10. Carry their rows into the scan as prior evidence.
