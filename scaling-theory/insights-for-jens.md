# Scaling Theory — insights for Jens

The notes cover the source only. Takeaways for Jens live here: recurring themes first, then one
entry per episode, newest first.

## Recurring themes

### 1. The cost of one attempt is the lever

- Growth comes from many cheap attempts and a few big wins (Cochrane, Gabaix). For a portfolio
  of small extensions: keep each launch cheap rather than polishing one.
- Any single listing, channel or post most likely lands in the long tail (Gabaix: audiences
  follow a power law). What matters is that a unit does not drop to zero, not chasing a hit.

### 2. A good product is not yet a public signal

- Pinker: coordination needs a signal everyone knows the others see. With no audience, a
  launch creates none. A visible rating count or user number is the closest a small extension
  gets.
- Howitt: his simulations worked, and nobody listened for 30 years. Method without distribution
  stays invisible.

### 3. AI review is a floor, and AI ideas are common property

- Cochrane: Claude passed his paper; a human colleague then tore three parts apart. AI gates
  catch the obvious, not the rest.
- Mollick: AI ideas beat people on average but look alike. What the models find, every other
  model user finds too. The edge has to come from own domain knowledge.
- Mollick: agents test whether software works, not how a buyer receives it. Few of the current
  gates ask the buyer's question.

### 4. Platforms that can say no

- Cochrane: whoever needs permission meets someone who "can just say no". The Chrome Web Store
  is such a gate; the risk sits in one place.
- Howitt: incumbents now copy before they buy. A paid extension on top of a platform's own
  feature is exposed to that.

## Per episode (newest first)

- **Mollick (20.10.):** decision logs and critic agents are his recipe for working out of the
  loop. The solo brake is review and distribution capacity, not agent output. Hidden steering
  text for AI readers backfired on his own book page.
- **Gabaix (28.09.):** size comes from compounding improvements, not from a lock-in moment. Pay
  follows the size of what the work affects, so freelance rates follow the client's business,
  not the hours.
- **Cochrane (31.08.):** his only known way into AI training data is publishing openly on the
  web; his Substack drew readers to his papers (on top of an existing reputation).
- **Howitt (27.07.):** more competition helps the firm at the frontier and discourages the
  laggard. Enter only where you can be at the front, not as the tenth copy.
- **Pinker (29.06.):** a correction made in public costs the corrected person authority (CC vs
  BCC). Relevant for answering client or user complaints in view of others.
