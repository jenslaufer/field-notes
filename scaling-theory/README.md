# Scaling Theory (Thibault Schrepel)

Podcast of the Network Law Review. Host Thibault Schrepel (antitrust law, Vrije Universiteit
Amsterdam) asks every guest what makes systems grow, what slows them down, and what AI changes.
Feed: https://anchor.fm/s/f3a5f9e4/podcast/rss

Covered here: **the unnumbered run that started in June 2026** (5 episodes, about 4 hours). The
feed has no season tags. Episodes up to #30 (May 2026) are numbered; this run is not. Episode #21
(Melanie Moses) is in [`../complexity/`](../complexity/2025-07-29-melanie-moses-scaling-decentralization.md).

Shared theme: growth comes from many cheap attempts, and a few of them win big. Each guest
names a different brake on those attempts: coordination (Pinker), incumbents (Howitt),
permission (Cochrane), firm size (Gabaix), and old processes (Mollick).

Method: every note comes from a local ASR transcript of the full audio (faster-whisper
`base.en`), not from the show notes. Every quote is checked with `tools/verify-quotes.py`
(exit 0 for all five). ASR mangles names ("Skating Theory", "Tibosh Kepen"). Garbled words are
quoted as transcribed and resolved in [brackets].

## Episodes

- [`2026-06-29-steven-pinker-common-knowledge.md`](2026-06-29-steven-pinker-common-knowledge.md) — Steven Pinker: people coordinate at scale through signals they know are public ("everyone knows that everyone knows"), not by modelling each other's minds; the same idea explains CC vs BCC, anonymous giving and communal outrage
- [`2026-07-27-peter-howitt-creative-destruction.md`](2026-07-27-peter-howitt-creative-destruction.md) — Peter Howitt (Nobel 2025): near the frontier more competition raises innovation, but incumbents escape competition through preemptive mergers, political power and by financing the AI firms that may displace them
- [`2026-08-31-john-cochrane-empty-skies.md`](2026-08-31-john-cochrane-empty-skies.md) — John H. Cochrane: growth comes from cheap attempts, so permission-by-default regimes (fracking, nuclear, light aviation: $80,000 → $800,000, "the skies are empty in Europe") cost more than any distribution fight; regulate retrospectively with sunsets
- [`2026-09-28-xavier-gabaix-shape-of-big-things.md`](2026-09-28-xavier-gabaix-shape-of-big-things.md) — Xavier Gabaix: random proportional growth gives cities, firms, CEO pay and podcast audiences the same Zipf shape, so giant firms are the normal outcome, and shocks to a few of them move a real share of GDP
- [`2026-10-20-ethan-mollick-coexisting-with-ai.md`](2026-10-20-ethan-mollick-coexisting-with-ai.md) — Ethan Mollick: handing AI whole tasks is a one-way door, firms gain nothing large until they reorganise, and being out of the loop is a question of evaluation design, not lost control
