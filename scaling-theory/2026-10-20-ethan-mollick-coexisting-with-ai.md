## 1. From co-intelligence to coexistence: delegation is one-way

- The intro frames the new book: *"we now hands"* [00:26] *"AI entire tasks and do not take them back."* [00:34]
- Mollick says the old mode still exists but recedes: *"It's just fading"* [03:25] *"in importance, right?"* [03:29]
- What he expects to hold: the human-side questions, because *"AI might change humans don't."* [03:43] — when human involvement matters *"either for social reasons or learning"* [03:48] *"reasons or societal reasons."* [03:52]
- The irreversibility claim: *"as you delegate things to AI,"* [03:56] *"things change in durable ways, right? We don't get that delegation back in some ways."* [04:03]
- His own example of a moved line: an early GPT-4 auto-reply tool for spam *"tended to over accept those emails."* [05:28] Now: *"I trust an AI to do that kind of spam email processing from me."* [05:43]
- The jagged frontier moves unpredictably; math was *"a clear weak spot"* [05:55] until *"the first Reesiter [reasoner] models, the 01 preview series"* [06:05] [o1-preview].
- Neither recursive self-improvement nor continual learning is operating yet; every chat is *"an amnesiac."* [07:01] *"It's just reading a prompt that you wrote before and incorporating that in."* [07:10]

## 2. The firm: gains wait for reorganization

- Schrepel cites the BCG study (*"over 700 consultants"* [07:45]): *"25% faster and at over 40% higher quality"* [07:51], but *"19% percentage points"* [07:59] worse on tasks outside the frontier.
- Mollick calls those papers *"already pre-agentric [pre-agentic] papers"* [09:08].
- Coding is the test case: coders write *"maybe 17 times more code"* [09:56], *"But that doesn't matter if you're part of a team that does two week long"* [10:01] *"spritz [sprints]"* [10:05] while marketing and sales run *"in a five-month cadence"* [10:15].
- Roles blur, and buyers want the wrong thing: *"They want AI to look like previous waves of technology"* [10:36]; *"This is a much deeper, more profound change than a SaaS solution in place."* [10:47]
- AI-run organizations are not *"pure science fiction at all."* [11:08] He reports companies running *"entirely autonomous AI systems"* [11:13] in parallel as a test, and: *"I had my agents trying to make me money"* [11:17] *"at one point and they successfully did, but it was my idea."* [11:22]
- The risk of full autonomy is monoculture: the systems are *"kind of like having one person or a small team of very"* [11:46] *"similar people run stuff."* [11:52] *"The AI makes very similar decisions. Variation is actually really important for this stuff as well."* [12:01]

## 3. Entrepreneurship: ideas are cheap, outliers still win

- The idea-generation study: *"35 of the top 40 product ideas came out of GPT-4"* [12:30]; a person with the model produces *"800 ideas per hour."* [12:37]
- Mollick's summary: *"AI generates more ideas"* [13:06] *"than people. On average, they're more creative than people. They're less diverse than people."* [13:11]
- Outliers keep their edge: *"if you're an outlier, right? And you're very good at this, you will beat the AI."* [13:21]
- His definition of the job: *"being in the top like 0.01% in something"* [13:36], *"And then desperately trying to make sure that all the other stuff you"* [13:43] *"have to do to run a company doesn't kill you"* [13:46] — the overhead AI now absorbs.
- He cites a study that *"something like 50% of Americans"* [14:02] *"who had a startup idea not pursued it."* [14:07]
- On ideation itself: *"I think you want the original"* [14:46] *"ideas yourself generally, otherwise you're going to come with a very AI-ish idea."* [14:54]
- Classroom result (companies launched in three days): *"The outlier ideas,"* [15:12] *"stayed around the same."* [15:17] The obviously doomed ones *"disappeared"* [15:21].

## 4. Out of the loop is a management problem, not a loss of control

- Schrepel raises the legal rule of a human in the loop; at billions of data entries a human *"can only validate the result, but you cannot reconstruct the whole analysis."* [24:22]
- Mollick: *"I think we overestimate"* [24:45] *"the nature of the kind of loops"* [24:50]; CEOs and senior partners sit in a loop only *"in a vague way, or at least imagine a loop"* [24:55].
- *"being out of the loop doesn't mean that you can't evaluate."* [25:23] *"It means we need to start being clever about how we're doing those evaluations."* [25:28]
- His own practice: he asks agents *"to keep interim records of decisions that were made."* [25:33] and to *"create artifacts and pieces along the way."* [25:38]
- A named bias: the models *"are very programming-brained"* [25:43] — good tests on whether software works, *"but not a lot about what the audience will receive it is."* [25:53] Remedy: *"run some independent agents pretending to be critics"* [25:57].
- The general claim: *"all work is delegation."* [23:17] and in-the-loop thinking is *"only one method of maintaining control."* [26:17]
- Law faces a *"threshold case"* [26:34]: *"there's an entire business model built around and approach about"* [26:38] *"what's scarce that's going to change."* [26:42]

## 5. Education: tutors outside class, evaluation inside, deep and wide knowledge

- AI tutoring works when used as a tutor; studies (incl. a large one in China) suggest *"it undermines learning if people are just using it to get help with homework."* [18:08]
- His forecast: AI tutors outside class, active learning inside. *"It won't be fast. It'll be messy."* [18:26]
- Cost collapse in his own work: what once took *"14 coders and raised a couple million"* [18:36] *"dollars in funding"* he *"can build in a day now."* [18:41]
- Research side: *"every every journal is overwhelmed by slop"* [18:48].
- The purpose that grows: *"deep knowledge"* [19:09] *"of wide knowledge"* [19:14] [deep and wide knowledge] — enough depth *"to work"* [19:14] *"with our autonomous agent"* [19:18], enough breadth to see opportunities *"as fields blur."* [19:22]
- On career choice: *"building flexibility in is more important than thinking"* [20:08] *"about like I'm betting on a job"* [20:12]. Even press-release writing, which he would have called fully automatable, may stay human because AI prose *"gets to a certain point that doesn't get better than that."* [20:42]
- Law-school AI bans read to him *"as a lack of"* [27:10] *"confidence in your current pedagogical approach"* [27:14].

## 6. Benchmarks, sycophancy and machine readers

- His private test: an *"original sociology paper,"* [34:13] — gather data, pre-register, write. The models *"got it quite good at that"* [34:24], but nobody cares about the result.
- The *"walk-a-mole [guacamole] drone"* [34:36] idea is goofy on purpose — *"We're outside the training data."* [34:45] It also *"teaches things like tic-a-pancy [sycophancy]. The AI just being"* [34:45] *"too nice to me about this idea."* [34:50]
- The otter test: from *"blurry love-crafted [Lovecraftian] images"* [35:10] three years ago to realistic local video *"in three minutes."* [35:26] Yet *"misinformation hasn't drowned"* [35:32] *"us. And yet there isn't yet the AI great movie."* [35:37]
- His one prompt: *"pick an area of your deepest expertise and ask the AI to do this autonomously."* [35:48] *"you need to find the"* [36:28] *"Jagged Frontier yourself and then push along that."* [36:32]
- Hidden instructions to AI readers backfired: he *"interviewed"* [37:34] *"50 AI agents about the book"* [37:40]; *"the more advanced models hated"* [37:40] *"my original page"* [37:46] — *"you're trying to prompt inject me"* [37:53].

## 7. Risk, two futures, and the inconvenient part

- Two futures in the book: *"the elevator in the eschatot [eschaton]."* [32:18] The elevator: abilities rise, *"but AI still remains jagged."* [32:23] The other: *"we reach this ASI super intelligence, and the world just"* [32:45] *"changes profoundly."* [32:50]
- On his own past optimism about the internet at the Media Lab: *"what an idiotic thing to think and retrospect"* [31:09]; *"the second order effects are complicated."* [31:13]
- The inconvenient part he wants remembered: *"the role of agency overall."* [38:51] If people keep good use cases *"hoarding those things"* [39:09] *"privately"* [39:13], *"people will use AI in ways that replace human labor, replace thinking"* [39:18].
- The intro's warning, confirmed at the end: the line that AI only replaces those who do not use it *"is just not true at this point."* [39:41]
