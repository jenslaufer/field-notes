# How to Stay Relevant as a Data Scientist in the Age of AI

> **Invest in what is hard to automate: judgment, stakeholder communication, messy domain
> expertise.** Krohn's ODSC West keynote: the task length LLMs handle doubles every seven months,
> but only where training data can be simulated and checked (math, code). Data science is messier,
> so humans keep more ground there. The coder's moat is gone; the new edge is framing the right
> context and questions, thinking like a product manager, and knowing the fundamentals well enough
> to defend an LLM's output to stakeholders.

## Insights

1. **The pace, quantified.** Krohn's keynote used charts from METR and artificialanalysis.ai. The
   task length LLMs *"can competently handle doubles every seven months and that stretches back
   many years now."*
2. **Why code and math fall first.** LLMs excel where there is *"a definitive right answer and you
   can simulate data effectively"*. Data science is messier: *"There's a bit more for us to do as
   humans still in that space."*
3. **Three skills to invest in.** *"judgment, stakeholder communication"* and *"messy, real world
   domain expertise."* Malone's synthesis, which he endorses: the value sits at the interface
   between the machine's internals and business problems.
4. **His work week now.** Much of it is *"thinking up what the right context is and what the right
   questions are"* for an agent or chat interface.
5. **The moat is gone.** He spent 20 years on Python, R, MATLAB, ML and statistics. Typing code by
   hand today means *"you'd really be shooting yourself in the foot"*.
6. **Move up the value chain.** *"even if you're a data scientist, you need to be thinking more
   like a product manager."* Ask what users, customers and internal stakeholders want, then use
   LLMs to do more. He claims mathematical and computer science employment rose since ChatGPT.
7. **Juniors are the open problem.** How do they build intuition when they can ask Claude for the
   answer? His hope: universities that force hand-written tests, and seniors who push juniors to
   think without LLMs in the right places.
8. **Fundamentals still win the hire.** Two candidates both use LLMs. Pick the one who understands
   what is *"under the hood"* and can say why an output makes sense. That person must later
   convince people of strategic decisions based on data or models.
9. **Learn by committing.** He writes books and courses for *"super selfish"* reasons: a deadline
   forces him to understand the material.
10. **Path in.** Any quantitative degree works (physics, neuroscience). You *"just need to be
    comfortable manipulating data and running it through models."* A PhD signals persistence, not
    intelligence; *"It's only 1% of people have a PhD."*
11. **Career arc.** Oxford neuroscience PhD (one year master's, three years PhD funding, finished
    on weekends while trading), then two years at a New York hedge fund he hated, then data
    science via a chance meeting in a pub. He now regrets leaving academia and holds adjunct posts
    at Tulane and Wilfrid Laurier.
12. **Humans plus AI beat agents for specialist work.** He hires human video editors and operations
    staff who use AI themselves, so he need not track their fields. Same logic as SaaS: rebuilding
    Salesforce or Slack in-house makes little sense at *"20 bucks a month per head"*.
13. **AI in his own podcast.** 104 episodes a year. LLMs write the web episode summary. Guest
    research runs through a human researcher with an *"agent process"* plus human curation. For
    the solo episodes (*"about a third"*) LLMs do the research *"because it's just so good and so
    fast."*
14. **Agent research hallucinates.** Malone's prep agent claimed Krohn does stand-up comedy and
    proposed several rounds of questions about it. It was wrong; one more research pass made it
    concede.
15. **Y Carrot** (play on "Y hat") is his AI consultancy and is hiring independent data
    scientists who can advise on strategy, prototype and ship models to production.
