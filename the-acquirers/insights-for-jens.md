## By episode

### 2025-10-23 · [Winning Long-Term Games and Ergodicity with Luca Dellanna](2025-10-23-luca-dellanna-ergodicity-long-term-games.md)

1. **A Kelly reading of the holdings has to be fractional and state the uncertainty on the edge.** Jens asked for a Kelly reading of the portfolio the morning of capture. The episode gives three independent reasons the raw number is wrong: inputs are single draws, not parameters; Kelly optimises the *median* with a live left tail; it assumes serial play, not a world of simultaneous bets. Any Kelly output should carry a fraction and the sentence about uncertainty on the edge, or it is the confident-formula failure he describes.
2. **The near-miss idea is the one to build with, and it is half-built.** Every watcher in `~/repos/assistant/tools/` is a near-miss counter: `entwurf-offen` (finished, never sent), `inbox-offen` (asked, never answered), `zusage-check` (promised, never reported). Each exists because a "got lucky" was noticed once. Dellanna's addition: **count them as a rate**, not just fix each one — near misses per week estimate when the real failure lands. The events exist; nobody has summed them.
3. **A live near miss from the day of capture.** The 0-byte draft: a `--fix` redirect emptied a finished message, both send gates reported green on the empty file, and it was caught only because it happened to carry a link. Exactly *"a terrible decision, but I got lucky."* It produced a fix (exit 2 on an empty draft). It has never been counted.
4. **Ask how others with the same goal failed — the extension work still studies winners.** It reads top listings and copies their shape. The inverse question is cheap on the Chrome Web Store: listings with rank and no revenue are visible, and three are Jens's own (Jigsaw at rank 2 and 0.00 USD in 187 days is the cleanest specimen).
5. **The portfolio-of-13 has the right shape and the wrong wiring.** His 11 books are a fat-tail portfolio. The 13 listings look identical — except **only 4 can take money**. A bet you cannot collect on is not a lottery ticket; it is a ticket you did not buy.
6. **The short-horizon warning lands on the stated goal — name it, don't argue it.** "5k/month within 4 weeks" is the artificially short horizon in his sense. His claim is not that it is too ambitious but that it *removes the good options*: the moves available in four weeks are the casino ones. His prescription is not to abandon it: run both horizons every month, a few things that pay now and at least one that pays nothing now. Distribution on already-built assets is the short half; trust and relationships are the long half, currently empty (no list, no audience, no network).
7. **"Build trust or consume trust" is a channel decision not yet made.** No newsletter exists yet. If one is built, the 6-month vs 10-year split is the whole design decision, made once, at the start.
